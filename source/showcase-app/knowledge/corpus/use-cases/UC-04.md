## UC-04 Driver drowsiness and attention

Driver drowsiness and attention. Standard: AIS-184 (DDAWS) · CMVR 125Q(1). Dates: 1 Oct 2027 new models · 1 Jan 2028 all models. Challenge: Approval is not a track test. The manufacturer must run at least ten participants, none from the development team, in day and night, generating ten or more true-positive or false-negative events, and report per-participant sensitivity. What Deepgrid supplies: An in-cab drowsiness pipeline delivered together with the study that makes it approvable. The differentiator is not the model — it is handing the integrator a completed documentation package instead of a demonstration. Steps: In-cab infrared camera into the module: eyelid closure, gaze, head pose and PERCLOS-class metrics, working at night; Fuse with vehicle signals — reduced steering micro-corrections paired with larger, faster ones, and lateral lane-position variability; Warn above KSS level 8; escalate visual then acoustic per the HMI specification; Run the ten-participant study and deliver metrics, thresholds, activation speed, weather limits and the statistical analysis. Buyer: Fleet safety manager · driver · OEM homologation engineer · testing-agency assessor Gate: A completed ten-participant day-and-night study with per-participant sensitivity and standard deviation, plus the assessable documentation package. Risk: Intra-city, school and double-decker buses are outside scope unless the maker opts in. Sizing against all M2/M3 volume overstates this, and an urban-bus fleet is the wrong first pilot.
