## UC-03 Blind-spot and moving-off information

Blind-spot and moving-off information. Standard: AIS-186 · AIS-187 · CMVR 125Q(2)–(3). Dates: 1 Oct 2027 new models · 1 Jan 2028 all models. Challenge: Two-wheeler riders were 46.2% of India’s 1,77,175 road deaths in 2024 and pedestrians 20.6%. AIS-186 demands an information signal 15 m before the expected collision point and forbids manual deactivation. What Deepgrid supplies: One India-trained perception core with two output contracts — near-field side and near-field front. Same silicon, same corpus, different detection geometry and signal timing. Steps: Side and front near-field cameras into the module; classify pedestrian, adult cyclist and child cyclist; Project turn geometry including the counter-turn; raise the signal at least 15 m before the collision point and hold it through the manoeuvre; Detect VRUs crossing at 3–5 km/h and cyclists parallel at 0–10 km/h inside the 3.7 m plane; hold through standstill until 15 m past the stopping point; Pass the false-information tests, including the traffic-sign case. Buyer: OEM cab and body engineer · Tier-1 HMI owner · fleet safety officer · driver Gate: A characterised near-field result against both test geometries, with the false-information cases run, from an independent agency rather than a bench demo. Risk: The signal cannot be switched off, so a high nuisance rate is permanently in the driver’s peripheral vision. UC-02 is a prerequisite, not a parallel track.
