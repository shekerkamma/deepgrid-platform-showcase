## Released against silicon, not against the forecast

₹55 Cr of blended capital: ₹45 Cr of equity ($4.79M) for 18.07% at a ₹204 Cr pre-money, plus a ₹10 Cr collateral-free CGTMSE facility for working capital. Dilution is tied only to the milestone that creates enterprise value. Of the equity, 66% goes to tapeout NRE and IP , 16% to a 10-FTE engineering team over 18 months, 8% to AIS-162/188 type approval through ARAI/ICAT. The $3.17M of NRE is not one commitment. It is four, and each is a point where the next cheque can be withheld: Prove the blocks on a shared MPW Compute and radar DSP blocks, 5 mm² each, 100 samples $138K Full-die MPW prototype The complete 57 mm² part plus five prototype wafers $232K Backend to GDSII Physical design, DFT, integration and sign-off — only after the blocks work $1.00M Dedicated production mask The largest single commitment, paid only once silicon exists $1.00M Controller and PHY IP take the remaining $0.8M. At volume the die costs $3.88 — $2.38 of wafer against 1,010 good die per 300 mm wafer, plus $1.50 of assembly and test. NRE breaks even at 174,908 chips against a plan that ships ~217,000 dies through FY2032, and the mask is cut for a ~500,000-die lifetime, which is the volume commitment that earns the $2,400 wafer price the whole cost stack depends on.

## Ten things to test, and where the documents disagree with each other

The materials argue their own risks rather than burying them, which is worth crediting. Three of the ten come from the company’s own claim-control ledger, and two are dated cash calls that appear in no allocation line — it already knows which headline numbers will not survive diligence, which is a good sign about the team and a bad sign about the memorandum. Ordered by what an investor acts on first. Structural Concentration — two products carry 64% of revenue: The truck kit (₹450 Cr, 40%) and the smart mirror (₹270 Cr, 24%) are the same family sold to overlapping buyers. A certification delay or a fitment bottleneck hits ₹720 Cr at once — more than the other thirteen lines combined. Structural The FPGA window, not the ASIC, is what the next two years are made of: Qualified volume silicon lands H1 2028 — after the all-models date — and OEM design-in runs 18 to 30 months. So line-fit revenue cannot come first, and every rupee before H1 2028 has to come from the FPGA module through government and PSU channels (UC-06). The plan’s own FY2027 line agrees: ₹1.7 Cr, FPGA only. An investor should underwrite the FPGA window explicitly rather than treating it as a run-up to the real business. ## Ten things to test, and where the documents disagree with each other

The provisionals were filed March 2026 (IN2026/DGR/001–015) and must become complete specifications within twelve months of the priority date. The memorandum lists that expiry among its own four reasons to move — and then allocates nothing to it. This is a hard, dated cash call on the IP the entire thesis rests on , landing inside the round’s first year, and it appears in no line of the use of funds. Unfunded The ISO 26262 assessor gates the highest-value lane and is not in the budget: No functional-safety pathway is published, and until an assessor is engaged a Tier-1 is comparing a certified incumbent with an uncertified newcomer — a gap no perception result closes. The company’s own 30-day plan lists engaging one as a procurement action, but the use of funds carries AIS-162/188 type approval only . Ask for the quote and the line item before the term sheet, not after. Claim control Three regulatory timelines are in circulation, and only one survives: The memorandum and the financial model both anchor demand to GSR 184(E) from April 2026 . The company’s own claim controls list “an April 2026 mandate” and “GSR 184(E) as final law” as banned claims , and the standards-level dates are 2027–2028. This is not pedantry: it moves first mandated revenue by roughly eighteen months, and it is the first thing a diligence team with a regulatory adviser will check.

## Ten things to test, and where the documents disagree with each other

Claim control The ₹1 Cr defence-revenue claim does not survive its own claim controls: It appears on the memorandum cover and in the model’s historical context. The internal ledger substantiates ₹23.01L delivered and ₹78.39L at L1 stage — lowest bidder, not an award — sitting in a different legal entity (Deepgrid Datacentre Pvt Ltd) with the IP owned by MCEME. The delivered revenue is real and is genuine proof the stack works before silicon; the headline is not the number to put in front of an investor. Document The two totals in circulation are ₹1,128 Cr and ₹1,388 Cr: They differ by ₹259.5 Cr across eleven product lines that the business plan carries and the financial model does not size. This is not only a document-to-document gap: the information memorandum’s own revenue slide is headed ₹1,128 Cr while its tables sum to ₹1,388 Cr. Pick one before the data room opens; the model is the conservative document and the one this summary follows. Document Tapeout NRE is quoted at both $2.42M and $3.17M: The die card says $2.42M; the platform slide says “a single $3.17M tapeout”; the model’s use-of-funds says $3.17M phased across four gates. A $750K difference in the single largest line item is the first thing a diligence team will price. Same for the addressable pool — 5M+ trucks in the memorandum against 1.0M in the model.

Claim control The compute-box line should not be underwritten on price: Published module pricing for embedded edge-AI parts does not support a price-advantage claim for the A100 SKUs, several of which carry fewer channels than comparably priced alternatives. The defensible positions are domestic supply, India-tuned perception and deployed-system cost — and a like-for-like deployed comparison is a diligence deliverable, not a slide. The company’s own claim controls bar quoting any competitor per-unit price, which the memorandum’s pricing slide does anyway. Structural Key-person exposure on both sides of the company: The founder is the single point of commercial accountability across all four business units and holds 95.6% of shares. The CTO — architect of the matrix ALU and author of the radar RTL — is not yet full-time , with the plan showing Q1 FY28. Separately, the cap table totals 102,797 shares against the 100,890 used in the pricing basis, a 1,907-share difference to reconcile before term sheet.

Two things that were flagged earlier and now resolve cleanly. The “six cameras” versus “eleven channels” question is a difference of scope, not an error: the AD2 kit is specified at six cameras, and the compute box ingests eleven channels (7 RGB + 2 thermal + 2 radar) in the same 8.6 ms. And prior capital is deployed, not banked : ₹48 lakh of grants (ARAI ₹36L, HDFC Parivartan ₹12L) and ~₹2 Cr of angel funding are fully spent, which is why the ₹2.5 lakh cash position is a date, not a number. The forecast is the consequence of those gates, not the thing being funded.
