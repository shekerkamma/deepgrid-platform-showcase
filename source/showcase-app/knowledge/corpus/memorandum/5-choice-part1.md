# DeepGrid Semi — memorandum/5-choice-part1

All figures are management projections from DeepGrid's fundraising materials.

## The company has a mandate it cannot yet serve and a chip that arrives after it

The strategic decision How does Deepgrid bridge the ~24 months between the mandate wave and its own qualified silicon — and which lane compounds into the ₹1,128 Cr plan rather than merely surviving the gap? Four constraints bound every answer. Cash is ₹2.5 lakh , so the first lane must produce revenue in months, not years. The 15 provisional patents carry a March 2027 conversion deadline that no use-of-funds line pays for. No ISO 26262 assessor is engaged , which gates the highest-value lane entirely. And the post-raise team is ten engineers with a CTO who is not yet full-time — enough to run two lanes properly, not five. The plan implies a path without ever arguing for it. That is the gap this section closes: five lanes were available, and the reason to pick one is not that it is the only one, but that it is the only one whose output is the next lane's input.

## Government and PSU wedge — sell the FPGA module where the gate is eligibility

List the module on GeM as a Class-I local-content perception kit and bid into state transport undertaking, PSU and municipal tenders. At ≥50% local content the company earns a 20% margin of purchase preference and a right to match. This is the only arena where the gate is eligibility , which Deepgrid can satisfy today, rather than certification, which it cannot. Upside: Revenue in months on hardware that already exists — and every delivered unit is an instrumented vehicle. Trade-off: Warning-only retrofit, price-competitive, and an incumbent already draws ~30% of its revenue from these tenders. What must be true: Local content on the real bill of materials clears 50%, and telemetry rights survive a public-procurement contract.

## Tier-1 attach — sell bounded perception into someone else’s safety case

Supply detection, classification, tracking and per-target time-to-collision on a documented interface to a braking Tier-1 already nominated on an OEM programme. Actuation, calibration, homologation and warranty stay with the Tier-1 — the division that makes the sale possible at all. Upside: The highest terminal value in the portfolio. This is the ₹450 Cr truck line and the platform prize behind it. Trade-off: Design-in runs 18–30 months, so the January 2027 date is already unwinnable and first revenue is FY2029. What must be true: An ISO 26262 assessor is engaged and funded — it appears in no use-of-funds line — and one Tier-1 records a written go.

## Defence-first — make the secure-compute chiplet the revenue engine

Lead with D100 and the D-HUMR platform through iDEX, DRDO and the MCEME pipeline, where indigenous-procurement rules exclude every import-dependent competitor by specification rather than by price. Upside: Near-zero competition, a procurement route independent of the automotive mandate, and a clean carve-out exit vector. Trade-off: ₹30 Cr of the plan — 2.7%. Defence telemetry produces nothing that transfers to road perception. What must be true: The IP position is resolved. Delivered revenue is ₹23.01L, it sits in a different legal entity, and the IP is MCEME-owned.

## Licence-first — sell the core instead of the product

Pull T100 IP licensing forward from FY2029: no bill of materials, no installer, no warranty logistics, 90%+ margin on every rupee. Upside: The cleanest economics in the plan and the only line that needs no operational capacity whatsoever. Trade-off: Nobody licences an unproven core, and licensing the core undercuts the systems business that is 88% of revenue. What must be true: Silicon has a track record first — which is precisely why the model already places this line in FY2029.

## Silicon sprint — spend the round on the tapeout and treat interim revenue as noise

Compress everything into reaching qualified silicon, accept that FY2027–28 revenue is immaterial, and run the company as a single-milestone entity until H1 2028. Upside: Fastest route to the asset that actually creates enterprise value, and the simplest story to tell. Trade-off: Money does not compress a fab schedule much, and a slip has no revenue underneath it to absorb the delay. What must be true: ₹45 Cr covers 24 months with no parallel revenue — and this is what the round already is if no lane is chosen deliberately. ## Run A for B, not A then B

Lead with the government wedge and open the Tier-1 exchange in the same month. The temptation is to read Option A as a way to survive until the real business starts. That is the wrong reading, and it costs the company its only open differentiator. The dossier is explicit that India-native perception is no longer a differentiator — four rivals already claim it. The one contest whose result is not yet written is the measured nuisance rate , because no competitor has published one. Producing that number needs instrumented vehicles running Indian roads with scenario tags applied at capture and telemetry rights secured in the contract. Option A is the only lane that generates exactly that — and the number it generates is the single strongest thing Deepgrid can put in front of the Tier-1 in Option B. So the sequencing is not defensive. Government units are the sensor network that produces the evidence that wins the design-in. Sequence them A-then-B and the data arrives too late to matter; run A for B and the pre-silicon window becomes the thing that makes the post-silicon business winnable.

## Run A for B, not A then B

Ring-fence C, defer D, reject E as a standalone. Defence stays a separate two-engineer lane with its own procurement route and its own exit vector — valuable precisely because it is uncorrelated with the mandate, and not a candidate to lead because its telemetry transfers nothing. Licensing stays where the model already puts it. And Option E is what this round becomes by default if no lane is chosen deliberately, which is the argument for choosing. Before committing

## Four questions that would change this recommendation

Each of these is an assumption the recommendation rests on, and none of them is validated in any document reviewed. Two carry a dated cash call that appears in no use-of-funds line. 01 Does the real bill of materials clear 50% local content? Class-I status is the entire basis of Option A, and no document reviewed computes it. Compute it on the actual BOM with imported content valued in, before the GeM listing is drafted. Unvalidated 02 Do telemetry rights survive a public-procurement contract? The evidence engine only exists if the data can be retained. Pull one state transport undertaking’s standard contract and check admissibility before the first bid, not after the first award. Unvalidated 03 What does an ISO 26262 assessor cost, and where is it funded from? It gates Option B entirely, the use-of-funds carries AIS-162/188 certification only, and the company’s own 30-day plan lists engaging one as a procurement action. Unfunded 04 Who pays to convert 15 provisional patents by March 2027? Provisionals must become complete specifications within twelve months of the March 2026 priority date. That is a hard, dated cash call on the IP the whole thesis rests on, and it appears in no allocation line. Unfunded · dated The business case

## The round clears self-funding by ₹10.1 crore, and both missing lines land inside it

Pricing the recommended path against the model’s own cash schedule produces one number that decides the round, and it is not the terminal year. Closing cash balance ₹ Cr · base case, from the model’s own runway schedule Axis capped at ₹50 Cr; the last two columns are clipped. The model’s verdict on this schedule is “funded to self-sustaining”, and on its own terms that is correct. But the trough is ₹10.098 Cr on a ₹45 Cr round — a 22% margin of safety, with no contingency line anywhere in the use of funds. The undrawn ₹10 Cr CGTMSE facility is not in this schedule, and is almost exactly the size of the trough. The decision threshold The round works if the two unfunded lines come in under roughly ₹10 crore combined and the CGTMSE facility is genuinely available to draw. Above that, this is a short round wearing a full one. Both lines land in FY2027–28, the same window as the ₹18 Cr and ₹10 Cr tapeout draws — patent conversion because the March 2027 deadline is fixed, and the functional-safety assessor because the Tier-1 exchange opens in month one. Neither can be deferred past the trough. Do not accept a term sheet before both are quoted.
