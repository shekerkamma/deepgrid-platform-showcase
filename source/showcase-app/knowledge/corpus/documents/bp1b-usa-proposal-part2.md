## Why that fourth column changes the plan. S1, S2 and S6 carry no longevity gate and no … (BP1B USA Proposal · p. 10)

Why that fourth column changes the plan. S1, S2 and S6 carry no longevity gate and no automotive silicon qualification at all — nobody demands a fifteen-year supply commitment on a soft-IP licence, a software model, or a private-land stack. S4, the packaged chip, is the biggest prize and the one thing we genuinely cannot sell into automotive yet. So the ladder is S2 and S6 first, S1 and S3 next, S4 last, and the honest conclusion is that our first American revenue is far more likely to be a licence than a part. I want to flag what that implies about the job, because it affects whether this partnership is the right fit rather than just an attractive one. Selling soft IP or a perception model to a Tier-1 is a long-cycle, technical, relationship-led enterprise sale — much closer to licensing than to distribution. If that is the kind of selling you have done, this works. If your strength is channel and volume distribution, then S6 — yard autonomy — is where you should start, and we should both know that going in. 6 What it costs to play in this category I would rather show you this than have you discover it.
