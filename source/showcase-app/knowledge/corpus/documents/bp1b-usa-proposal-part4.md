I agree with it, I think it is better than anything I had written, and it fixes the one real hole in this document: everything up to §9 ends at “gated on Series A” with no American capital plan at all. So here is that plan, with the parts I would push back on marked as such. Why the reference asset changes our position Today I walk into an American conversation with measured silicon and a good story. In 2028, if India goes the way BP-1A says it should, I walk in with something categorically different — a deployed fleet, field-failure data from a punishing duty cycle, and manufacturing maturity somebody else has already paid to prove. No amount of pitching substitutes for that, and it is the one asset the companies in §7 cannot simply buy with funding. But I want to be precise about what it proves, because overselling this is how the document would lose its credibility: THE INDIA FLEET PROVES IT DOES NOT PROVE Reliability at a hostile duty cycle — heat, vibration, dust, US regulatory compliance. AIS certification is not voltage transients, tamper resistance over real months FMVSS, and the test protocols differ Manufacturing and field maturity — that we can build, US highway perception validation.
