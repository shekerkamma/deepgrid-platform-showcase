Second: government/PSU/defence (§3 Customer 2); the GeM record CONTRACTED opens doors. Third — the mandate channel, reframed as upside, not a committed date. OEM/Tier-1 design-in is relationship-gated: comparable regulation-created hardware markets (EU tachograph, eCall) show mandate volume normally flows to whichever supplier already has OEM relationships and homologation experience, not the cheapest new entrant — those incumbents typically absorbed aftermarket share within ~18 months once line-fit normalized EXTERNAL REFERENCE . This plan therefore pursues OEM/Tier-1 engagement opportunistically from month one (a) AIS-162/188 certification (T2-funded, §9) PLAN , (b) retrofit field- hours and Indian-road data as validation, (c) the T2 milestone clock (§9) — but does not treat a signed design-in as a required, dated event the way §8's revenue trajectory implicitly assumes. The pitch leads with capability, not price: owning perception + compute + model on Jiyu's <100 ms ASIL-D EHB lets us certify a single photon-to-brake latency number no mix-and-match integration can produce — but that pitch needs an actual OEM contact, which this plan does not yet have (§10 risk 10).

## Fast case vs. slow case. The plan as financially modeled (§8) is a fast case: … (BP1A India Autonomous Trucking Plan · p. 15)

Fast case vs. slow case. The plan as financially modeled (§8) is a fast case: certification, tapeout and first OEM engagement all land inside 12–18 months. Comparable mandatedriven hardware markets have historically taken 3–5 years to move from mandate announcement to stable incumbent share EXTERNAL REFERENCE . In the slow case, FY27– FY29 revenue rests almost entirely on government/defence and insurer-co-funded retrofit (this section) while certification and OEM engagement continue in the background with no committed date — the fast case is upside, not the base the company should be funded against. The three phases are the spine of this plan, so they are set out as a matrix rather than described in prose. Read Figure 8 as the answer to four questions per phase: who we sell to, through whom, at what price, and what has to be true before the next phase opens.
