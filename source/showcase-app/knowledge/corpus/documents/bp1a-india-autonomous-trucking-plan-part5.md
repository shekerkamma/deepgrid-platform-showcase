## The uncomfortable reading, kept in deliberately. Against imports we are three times … (BP1A India Autonomous Trucking Plan · p. 24)

The uncomfortable reading, kept in deliberately. Against imports we are three times cheaper. Against the device that actually wins mandated volume in India we are twenty times more expensive. Both statements are true, and a serious investor will find the second one. Our answer has to be that we are not selling into the same job: a ₹5,000 box satisfies an inspector, while our kit is what an OEM can put on a production line and a fleet can build a safety case on. That answer only holds if (a) enforcement eventually becomes real, (b) we win OEM line-fit, and (c) we field a stripped SKU for the segment that only wants compliance (§6). If none of those three lands, the low end takes the mandate and we are left with the organised segment alone. Moat, in order of durability: 1. Structural price floor: owned silicon at <$3–4/bare die (₹1,750 packaged, §5) makes the ₹2.3L price ours to hold; importers cannot follow down without destroying their own margin stack. 2. Certification + sovereignty: claimed only-indigenous AIS-validated chipset, AIS-004 native, 15 patents filed; foreign silicon excluded from government/defence fleets (§2). Certification is a path, not held — the killer risk, §10. 3. Data moat that compounds — narrower than it sounds.
