## Stage 2 — Gap ranking and fill — DeepGrid Semi IM v2

Stage 1 audited the document against itself. This stage checks it against the world. Tool order followed: local repo files first, then GBrain recall, then Exa. Local and GBrain answered most of it — this engagement has seven prior DeepGrid runs and four compiled GBrain pages, and they had already verified the two facts that matter most. Headline: the IM's central "Why Now" is false, and prior work in this repo told them so before this IM was written.

## C1. The mandate the whole thesis rests on is a draft that never became law

The IM's first reason to invest: "MoRTH GSR 184(E) enforcement is live — April 2026. Every N2/N3 truck sold after this date requires ADAS compliance… Regulatory floor, not a forecast." It repeats April 2026 on p.3, p.6, p.7 and p.26. G.S.R. 184(E) (20 Mar 2025) was only ever a draft notification. The notified rule is G.S.R. 834(E), 11 Nov 2025, in force 12 Nov 2025, amended by G.S.R. 862(E) of 21 Nov 2025. The binding dates are: System Standard New models All models AEBS + stability AIS-162 / IS 11852:2019 1 Jan 2027 1 Oct 2027 LDWS AIS-188 1 Oct 2027 1 Jan 2028 DDAWS / BSIS / MOIS AIS-184/186/187 (rule 125Q) 1 Oct 2027 1 Jan 2028 Independently corroborated outside this repo: Fortune India reports AEBS and stability control mandatory from 1 October 2027, and BSIS/MOIS/DDAWS/LDWS from 1 January 2028, notified vide GSR 834(E) dated 11 November 2025. MoRTH's own AIS register still lists AIS-162 with status "Draft". So there is no April 2026 mandate. There is no "regulatory floor" already live. ## C2. The ₹1 Cr defence revenue is misdescribed four ways

The IM states "₹1Cr Defence Revenue" on the cover, "₹1 Cr institutional defence revenue RECEIVED Q1 2026" on p.15, "₹1Cr Defence Revenue Received" as a completed milestone on p.18, and "₹1 Cr defence revenue already received" in the exit vectors on p.26. Prior verified work finds: ₹23.01L delivered (GeM Robot Training); ₹78.39L is L1 — lowest bidder, not an award; it sits in Deepgrid Datacentre Pvt Ltd, not Deepgrid Semi; and IP on the delivered contract is MCEME-owned under Clause 27. The company's own BP-1A states "none of it is trucking/ADAS revenue." Four separate problems in one number: 78% of it is not revenue, it is in a different legal entity than the one raising, the IP does not belong to the company, and none of it is ADAS. A single diligence question retires it.

## C3. The competitor set omits every threat that prior work ranked highest

The IM names Mobileye, Bosch, Steradian, Horizon Robotics, Nvidia, Netrasemi, NXP, Mindgrove, Aurora, Kodiak, ABB/Kalmar, ideaForge, Apptronik and Agility, and asserts on p.19 that Indian competition is a "genuine open lane… the one unit where 'no Indian competitor' is unambiguously true." Not named, and ranked as the real threats: - ZF WABCO — ADAS already shipping as OE fitment with a leading Indian CV OEM, with an in-house Indian test track and homologation capability DeepGrid concedes it lacks. Prior work calls this the actual threat, "not Nvidia/Mobileye." - Starkenn — company-reported 2,500+ vehicles and 30% of revenue from government tenders, plus BrakeSafe radar AEBS; highest threat in the government/mining segment the IM treats as open. - drivebuddyAI — $2.5M contracted across 3,000 heavy trucks (May 2026), scope to 10,000. No longer demo-only. - bitsensing — India retrofit ADAS kit launched Feb 2026 with an IKIO channel MoU. - Gahan AI, Aptiv, STRADVISION, Sterling Tools × MINIEYE. Netrasemi's ₹107 Cr Series A also demotes "India-native" as a differentiator — domestic origin alone no longer distinguishes DeepGrid.

## C4. The import price column cannot be sourced, and quoting it was already banned

p.14 prices Mobileye at ₹5–8L, Continental ₹8–12L, Nvidia ₹12–18L, Qualcomm ₹10–15L, footnoted only as "public product specifications" with no citation. No public per-unit India price exists for Mobileye EyeQ6, Bosch/Continental, Qualcomm SA8295 or Nvidia Orin truck ADAS. Six of 27 rows in a prior evidence ledger are UNVERIFIED and all six are import prices; bitsensing's "pricing agreed case by case" corroborates that this is market structure, not a search failure — automotive ADAS unit pricing is quoted, not listed. A prior deck made quoting such a figure an explicit banned action. Stage 1 already showed the "3 to 7×" claim contradicts its own table (the range is 2.0×–9.0×). Loaded with NRE at plan volumes, prior work puts the real advantage at ~2.3×, and notes that the same category error — comparing a competitor ASP to DeepGrid's die cost — is what forced an earlier "774×" claim to be retracted.

## C5. The 88% gross margin is arithmetically unreachable

The IM projects 88% blended GM at FY2032. Prior analysis: 88.4% blended requires ~120% margin on all non-kit revenue. At 72% kit margin the honest blend is 77.2%. An 11-point GM error at FY2032 revenue of ₹1,388 Cr is roughly ₹150 Cr of gross profit, which flows straight to the ₹536.6 Cr EBITDA line the deck leads with.

## What the IM gets right

Worth stating, because it is not all bad and one item is a genuine improvement over later company material. - 39.3 TOPS is correctly labelled a derivation, not a measurement. The Artix-7 board ceilings near 1.8 TOPS, and a later July deck wrongly upgraded this figure to "measured." This June IM does not make that error. - The 130nm SkyWater validation tapeout is disclosed rather than omitted. - The MicroDC-A1 roadmap slide carries an explicit conceptual-product disclaimer. - The P&L is footnoted as management estimates, not independently audited.

## Limitations

It is well-evidenced and gazette-checked, but it is our own file. - Only two claims were checked against sources outside this engagement in this run: the mandate timetable and the AIS-162 draft status. - The cap table, valuation, historical financials and runway remain unobtainable from any source available here — they are internal documents that do not exist in the IM. Stage 1 gaps 1, 2, 4, 5 and 6 are still open and no research closes them. - Competitor per-unit India pricing is not merely missing but structurally unavailable.
