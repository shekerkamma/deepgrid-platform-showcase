# Narration script — DeepGrid Semi financial model walkthrough

For Google Vids AI voiceover. One block per slide, written to be read aloud.
Target pace ~135 words/minute. Total ≈ 1,180 words ≈ 8m 45s.

Tone: measured, factual, first-person plural. Every claim is a projection and the
script says so where it matters. No superlatives.

---

**01 · Cover**
DeepGrid Semi. A walkthrough of our financial plan, prepared for a pre-Series A raise
of forty-five crore in equity alongside a ten crore collateral-free debt facility.
Every figure you will see is a management projection, not a reported result.

**02 · Executive summary**
The plan reaches self-funding on this round. One twenty-eight nanometre chip serves four
markets. Revenue is projected at eleven hundred and twenty-eight crore by financial year
thirty-two, at a thirty-four point four percent EBITDA margin. We turn EBITDA-positive in
twenty-twenty-eight, a full year before the projected cash low point of ten point one crore.

**03 · The ask**
Forty-five crore of equity at a two hundred and four crore pre-money valuation, giving an
investor eighteen point zero seven percent. Alongside it, a ten crore CGTMSE-backed facility.
The structure is deliberate: equity funds the silicon, debt funds the working-capital cycle.

**04 · Market**
Indian regulation converts advanced driver assistance from an optional purchase into a
required one. Around one million commercial vehicles a year fall in scope — half a million
new build, half a million retrofit. Our plan captures one point eight percent of that pool.

**05 · Platform**
The same chip and the same software stack ship under different firmware across road autonomy,
silicon and compute, fleet, and sensors and robotics. That is why one tapeout can support four
revenue lines rather than four separate engineering programmes.

**06 · Part one: the revenue plan**
First, how volume, price and customer count build to the projection.

**07 · Revenue**
Revenue is built bottom-up: every line is units multiplied by price. No total is set as a
target and back-solved. The plan runs from one point seven crore to eleven hundred and
twenty-eight crore across six years, with the inflection in twenty-twenty-eight.

**08 · Revenue mix**
Road autonomy carries two-thirds of the projection. That concentration is deliberate, because
mandated demand is the largest and most predictable pool — but it also means the two truck and
mirror ramps are the assumptions that most affect the outcome.

**09 · Business mix**
Systems, not standalone chip sales, are the economic engine — eighty-three percent of projected
revenue. The silicon creates the cost advantage; the revenue is realised by selling complete,
certified systems built around it.

**10 · Unit economics**
Eleven product lines, each priced independently. Prices are held flat across the plan, so growth
comes from volume rather than from price increases. The truck kit and the smart mirror together
represent seven hundred and twenty crore.

**11 · Customers**
Accounts grow from five to six hundred and fifty. Average revenue per account peaks in
twenty-twenty-nine and then falls as the base broadens into retrofit and fleet channels —
which is what removes single-account dependency from the plan.

**12 · Part two: margin and profitability**
Next, how gross margin expands and overhead ratios decline.

**13 · Margin architecture**
Each segment carries its own margin path. The blended margin is an output of the mix, not an
assumption applied to the top line. The expansion comes from the chip displacing a bought-in
module inside every kit.

**14 · Profit and loss**
Revenue, gross profit and every expense line derive from the same build. Gross margin rises
from thirty-nine point seven percent to eighty-seven point four. EBITDA turns positive in
twenty-twenty-eight and reaches three hundred and eighty-eight crore by thirty-two.

**15 · Operating leverage**
The step from loss to structural profitability happens once revenue clears the fixed
engineering base. Net margin reaches twenty-four point three percent in the terminal year.

**16 · Cost discipline**
All three overhead ratios decline as revenue scales. Research and development stays high by
design at thirty-three percent, because it carries the next silicon generation rather than
only sustaining the current one.

**17 · Research investment**
About three hundred crore of cumulative research spending funds the next five-nanometre chip
from operating profit. Read this correctly: that spending is an expense inside the projections
you have seen, not an addition to them.

**18 · Part three: silicon and capital**
Now what the round funds, what the chip costs, and when it pays back.

**19 · Use of funds**
Fifty-five crore in total. Silicon takes fifty-four percent, and equals sixty-six percent of
the equity round on its own. Working capital sits on the facility, not on equity.

**20 · Silicon programme**
The tapeout is phased so the largest commitment comes last. Blocks are proven on shared wafers
first; the production mask is ordered only once silicon works. Most of the spend is gated on
evidence rather than on schedule.

**21 · Chip economics**
At volume the die costs three dollars eighty-eight, derived from die size, wafer price and
yield. The programme recovers its one-time cost at about a hundred and seventy-five thousand
chips, well inside the plan horizon.

**22 · Schedule risk**
If the next-generation chip slips twelve months, the plan loses about fifty-eight crore in the
terminal year. The mandated truck and mirror business is unaffected, because it runs on
current-generation silicon.

**23 · Cash and runway**
Cash falls to a low point of ten point one crore in twenty-twenty-nine, then recovers as
silicon spending completes and operating profit outpaces working-capital growth. No further
equity round is assumed.

**24 · Capital structure**
Equity funds the asset; debt funds the cycle. Indian commercial-vehicle customers settle in
sixty to ninety days, and working capital absorbs eighty crore in the terminal year alone —
a cost that should not consume equity.

**25 · Ownership and prior capital**
The company is founder-controlled, with two point four eight crore of grant and angel capital
already deployed. For an incoming investor, governance rights matter more here than the
ownership percentage.

**26 · Operating plan**
Headcount grows from thirty-four to five hundred and forty-one, weighted to engineering.
We present it as the operating shape of the plan, and we reconcile it to the expense ratios
before close rather than merging the two now.

**27 · Part four: risk and decision**
Finally, the downside cases and what happens next.

**28 · Scenarios**
Each case is driven by one revenue multiplier and one overhead adjustment, so the downside is
testable rather than asserted. Even on a seventy percent revenue miss, the plan self-funds
without a further equity round.

**29 · What determines the outcome**
Five assumptions carry the projection: the two unit ramps, systems gross margin, yield and
wafer cost, working-capital intensity, and the silicon schedule. Against each, we are
assembling the evidence a diligence team would ask for.

**30 · Close**
We are raising forty-five crore to fund working silicon, paired with a ten crore non-dilutive
facility, against a plan that reaches self-funding without a further round. We would rather be
measured on verifiable gates — working silicon, certification, and paying customers — than on
the terminal year.
