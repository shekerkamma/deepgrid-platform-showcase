The proven middle ground is per-core DMA with shared L2

DGrid today (left of spectrum) is where most academic prototypes start: one DMA engine, simple to verify, hits a wall fast.

Tesla Dojo (right of spectrum) is the opposite extreme: each core gets its own SRAM, no DRAM at all. It works for one workload (vision training) at one scale (datacenter), with one customer (Tesla). Project was disbanded August 2025 after struggling to find general applicability.

Mobileye, NVIDIA, and Hailo all sit between these poles. They use per-core or per-cluster DMA against shared memory — exactly what DGrid's per-PCORE LDMA proposal would deliver. This is the architecture that ships in cars, security cameras, and edge devices today.

DGrid's roadmap from SoC1.2 → SoC1.3 → SoC2 traces this exact arc. Not radical. Not novel. Proven.
