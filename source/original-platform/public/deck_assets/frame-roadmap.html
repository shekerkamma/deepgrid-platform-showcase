Chapter A

From flat to cube — same workload, 8× faster

Both units compute the same K=8 reduction. The 8×8 matrix unit (SoC1.2) needs 8 cycles to consume K. The 8×8×8 cube (SoC2) consumes K in one cycle by stacking 8 multiply layers. Same answer, different geometry.

Same arithmetic, different geometry

The 8×8 unit reuses 64 weights across 8 cycles of streamed activations. The 8×8×8 cube embeds K=8 directly in silicon — every layer of weights and every K-slice of activations meet at the same cycle. 8× the parallel reductions, 8× the bandwidth needed.

Chapter B

The cube alone is not enough — it needs per-PCORE DMA

A cube can do 512 MACs per cycle. But only if data arrives at 512 bytes/cycle. The same 56 cubes fed by SoC1.x's single global DP would starve. Each cube would compute ~12% of cycles, the rest waiting. Watch what happens.

What this means for the SoC roadmap — three coupled decisions

The leap from SoC1.2 to SoC2 isn't just "more silicon." Three things move together — and they have to, because each enables the next.

01 — COMPUTE

Flat → Cube

8×8 (64 MACs) at SoC1.2. 8×8×8 (512 MACs) at SoC2. K becomes hardware depth, not iteration count.

02 — MEMORY

Shared DP → per-PCORE LDMA

Single global engine cannot feed 56 cubes. Each cube needs its own DMA against banked L2.

03 — SILICON

FPGA → 28nm ASIC

The cube + LDMA combination doesn't fit on FPGA. Custom 28nm gives the area, power, and clock for 600 MHz.

SoC1.2 ships the flat matrix on FPGA — proves the geometry. SoC1.3 prototypes per-PCORE LDMA on 1-2 cores — proves the memory architecture. SoC2 fuses both into the cube on 28nm — proves the production chip. Each step de-risks the next.
