**01 · Cover**
DeepGrid Semi. Fifteen product SKUs, built on one chip. This is where our eleven hundred
and twenty-eight crore revenue plan comes from, and why the decision in front of you is
simpler than a fifteen-product portfolio makes it look.

**02 · The case**
Our portfolio is a consequence of the silicon, not a strategy you have to believe fifteen
times over. Every SKU runs the same twenty-eight nanometre chip under different firmware.
Two SKUs carry sixty-four percent of the plan, and every line sits under two percent of its
own demand pool.

**03 · Why now**
India now requires drowsiness, blind-spot and lane-departure systems on commercial vehicles.
New models from April twenty-twenty-six, the existing fleet from October. That is a pool of
one million trucks a year that must comply. The buying decision is when, not whether.

**04 · The gap**
Today that obligation is met with imported compute costing three to eight thousand dollars a
module. Our die costs three dollars and eighty-eight cents at volume. Read that honestly — a
bare die is not a finished module — but the gap is the opening, and it is why a locally
designed part can be priced for this market.

**05 · The move**
The round funds one thing: a phased twenty-eight nanometre programme. Three point one seven
million dollars, in four gated stages. We prove the compute and radar blocks on a shared
wafer, then the full die, and we commit to the production mask only once silicon works.

**06 · The portfolio**
Fifteen SKUs across four platform surfaces. Prices run from five thousand rupees for the
driver-monitor wearable to one crore for the perception licence. Same silicon underneath all
of it, priced independently by the market each one serves.

**07 · Concentration**
Two SKUs carry sixty-four percent. The Smart Truck kit at four hundred and fifty crore, the
Smart Mirror at two hundred and seventy. They are the same product family sold to overlapping
buyers, so a certification or installation delay hits seven hundred and twenty crore at once.
That single exposure is larger than the other thirteen SKUs combined.

**08 · Sequencing**
Six SKUs earn revenue in financial year twenty-seven, on FPGA hardware, totalling one point
seven crore. Nine more switch on the following year as ASIC-era product reaches market, and
the licence line begins in twenty-nine. The ramp is gated by silicon — which is precisely
what this round pays for.

**09 · Where autonomy runs**
The same chip operates in three domains. Outdoor carries eighty-six percent of revenue and
depends on homologation and public-road rules. Indoor is geofenced, so level-four behaviour
ships years earlier. Compute carries no vehicle exposure at all — it is silicon sold as
silicon.

**10 · Road autonomy**
Three kits, seven hundred and sixty-two crore, eighty-nine percent gross margin. The Smart
Mirror is the entry product and the volume ramp. The Smart Truck kit is the mandated core.
The indoor level-four kit removes traffic and homologation from the problem entirely.

**11 · Smart Mirror**
Fifty-four thousand units by twenty-thirty-two at fifty thousand rupees, giving two hundred
and seventy crore. This one is not mandated — it is a voluntary safety and convenience
purchase in a market already worth three point one billion dollars, where Maruti and Tata are
fitting surround-view today. What has to be true is channel reach, price holding, and the
existing sales base being real.

**12 · Smart Truck**
Eighteen thousand units at two and a half lakh, giving four hundred and fifty crore — the
largest SKU in the plan. It answers a legal obligation on N2 and N3 vehicles, so the buyer
does not need persuading that safety is worth paying for. Three things must land:
certification through ARAI or ICAT, installation capacity at scale, and fleet access.

**13 · Selling the chip, part one**
The highest-margin revenue we have. The bare die sold to other manufacturers at ninety-four
percent gross margin, needing no integration, certification or installation. Alongside it,
the entry and mid compute modules — the cheapest way for a customer to adopt the silicon.

**14 · Selling the chip, part two**
The four-channel PCIe card is our highest-priced compute SKU at three lakh, and the closest
the module line comes to a full system. The perception licence carries fifty crore on pure
margin — but it has no separately sized demand pool behind it, so treat it as optionality
rather than base case.

**15 · Fleet and mobility**
Two service-model lines with the highest revenue per unit in the portfolio. Autonomous
transport-as-a-service at sixty-six lakh a vehicle, debt-financed against the trucks rather
than funded from equity. And port autonomy, where we already hold a live order at Vizag
Container Terminal.

**16 · Sensors, part one**
Fifty percent gross margin — the lowest in the portfolio, and we state that plainly. Thermal
and radar are carried for capability: Indian night and fog are exactly where camera-only
systems fail. The driver-monitor wearable at five thousand rupees is the easiest first
purchase a fleet can make.

**17 · Sensors, part two**
The defence ground vehicle is the only line in this deck with revenue already received — one
crore, from the Army, on FPGA hardware. The drone kit opens a second procurement cycle for
very little incremental engineering, because it runs the same perception and compute.

**18 · Margin**
Volume and margin sit in different places. Systems are eighty-three percent of revenue at
eighty-nine percent margin, so group economics track that line. But the marginal rupee is
worth most in semiconductors at ninety-four percent, and those SKUs need no installation
capacity at all.

**19 · Headroom**
No SKU in this plan assumes we win its market. Every line with a sized pool sits under two
percent of it, and most of those pools are global while our serviceable market is the India
slice. These are floors, not stretches.

**20 · The ask**
Fund the silicon, and the portfolio follows. Fifteen SKUs, one tapeout. We would rather be
measured against verifiable gates — working silicon, certification achieved, and pilots
converted to paying customers — than against a terminal-year forecast. The portfolio is not
fifteen bets. It is one bet, fifteen times over.
