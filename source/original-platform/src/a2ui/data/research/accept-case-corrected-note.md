**Subject: DeepGrid — what the offer is worth to you**

Shravan,

Thank you for the time, and for pressing on fragmentation, pricing and risk. Here is the offer set out plainly.

**The investment.** ₹9.40 Cr at a ₹112 Cr pre-money gives you 7.74% of the Indian parent, diluting to 6.34% once the ₹45 Cr round closes. On the plan, FY32 revenue is ₹1,388 Cr at a 72% kit margin, giving ₹309 Cr of EBITDA — ₹19.6 Cr attributable to you, annually. At a 6–10× exit the stake is worth ₹117–196 Cr, which is 8.5–14× your total commitment including the eighteen months you would put in.

The volume behind that is modest by design: 18,000 truck kits at ₹2.50L, or 0.36% of the mandated fleet. Team overheads are covered by a few hundred kits a year. We do not need a heroic share of anything.

**The US entity.** Co-founder equity across four tranches, the first vesting at signing rather than after a probation period, with pro-rata rights and adjustment protection if the round moves. The Indian parent holds majority and licenses the IP; you hold real founder shares in the company you build.

**Why this is a co-founder's equity and not a salary.** The engineering is measured and shipping — perception running at 40 fps on our own silicon, fifteen patents filed, a government procurement record held that excludes foreign silicon from the segment. What does not exist is the commercial function: no pipeline, no CRM, one segment we can sell into today. I can build the part; I cannot get the first meeting.

That gap is the seat, and it is the one part of this business where twenty-three years of ISV and large-deal experience compounds immediately. Two of your four vesting tranches are introductions only you can make.

**Why the two halves pay you twice.** Once Indian trucks are running with our ADAS, that fleet becomes the reference that opens America — and it is the smaller US OEMs, not the majors, who will want it first. It is the one asset our better-funded peers cannot buy. The work you would do in India is what makes the US round raisable, and the US round is your fourth tranche.

**Before you subscribe, I will provide:** a valuation certificate and fair-value representation supporting ₹112 Cr, with a written explanation of the pricing in the concurrent round; evidence that the ₹45 Cr round is substantially committed, since it funds the tapeout and your subscription does not; the exclusive US licence terms in writing — scope, duration, exclusivity and change of control; and the US equity percentage named rather than left to later negotiation. Single tranche throughout. Please establish your residency position before we fix the structure.

I would value your read on the sequencing, and on where you think I have this wrong — in month one, not month ten.

Aravind
