![<p>The move is expected to offer greater flexibility to manufacturers, especially as vehicles increasingly integrate external safety sensors and ADAS architecture.</p>](https://etimg.etb2bimg.com/photo/125640779.cms)The move is expected to offer greater flexibility to manufacturers, especially as vehicles increasingly integrate external safety sensors and ADAS architecture.New Delhi: The Ministry of Road Transport and Highways (MoRTH) has issued significant amendments to India’s motor vehicle regulations, revising norms for how vehicle width is measured and introducing mandatory safety technologies for medium and heavy commercial vehicles. The updated rules, notified in the Gazette on 21 November 2025, replace provisions announced earlier this month and set phased compliance deadlines through 2028.

**External fittings excluded from width measurement**

Under the revised guidelines, several external components will no longer be counted when measuring the overall width of a motor vehicle. These include rear-view mirrors, devices for indirect vision, guard rails, direction indicators, side foot steps, and rubber beading up to 20 mm per side. Components associated with advanced driver assistance—such as sensors and housings for Blind Spot Information Systems (BSIS) and Moving-Off Information Systems (MOIS)—are also excluded, provided they remain within prescribed size limits.

The move is expected to offer greater flexibility to manufacturers, especially as vehicles increasingly integrate external safety sensors and ADAS architecture.

**Stability and braking systems to become mandatory**

MoRTH has also set clear deadlines for introducing core electronic safety systems across the M2, M3, N2 and N3 categories (buses and goods vehicles). All models in these segments must be equipped with [Vehicle Stability Function](https://auto.economictimes.indiatimes.com/tag/vehicle+stability+function) (VSF) and [Advanced Emergency Braking Systems](https://auto.economictimes.indiatimes.com/tag/advanced+emergency+braking+systems) (AEBS) within the following timelines:

- **New models:** 1 January 2027

- **Existing models:** 1 October 2027


Industry experts say the mandate aligns India with global [commercial vehicle safety standards](https://auto.economictimes.indiatimes.com/tag/commercial+vehicle+safety+standards), reducing rollover and collision risks in heavy vehicles.

**Lane departure warning and driver-assistance tech from 2027–28**

In addition, Lane Departure Warning Systems (LDWS) will become compulsory across the same categories from:

- **1 October 2027** (new models)

- **1 January 2028** (existing models)


A new rule also mandates advanced driver-monitoring and visibility systems—including Driver Drowsiness and Attention Warning, Blind Spot Information, and Moving-Off Information Systems. These must be installed in all new M2, M3, N2 and N3 vehicles from October 2027, with existing models brought into compliance by January 2028.

The government’s move signals a broader shift towards harmonising India’s safety regime with that of Europe and improving road safety outcomes across commercial fleets. Manufacturers are expected to accelerate platform upgrades to meet the phased deadlines, while operators may face marginal cost increases as advanced systems become standardised across segments.
