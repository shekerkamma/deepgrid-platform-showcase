## CRITICAL — must be fixed before the IM goes to any investor

### C1. The mandate the whole thesis rests on is a draft that never became law

The IM's first reason to invest: "MoRTH GSR 184(E) enforcement is live — April 2026. Every [S4] [S6]
N2/N3 truck sold after this date requires ADAS compliance… Regulatory floor, not a
forecast." [S1] It repeats April 2026 on p.3, p.6, p.7 and p.26. [S1]

**G.S.R. 184(E) (20 Mar 2025) was only ever a draft notification.** [S4][S9] The notified
rule is **G.S.R. 834(E), 11 Nov 2025**, in force 12 Nov 2025, amended by **G.S.R. 862(E)** [S4] [S9]
of 21 Nov 2025. [S4] The binding dates are:

| System | Standard | New models | All models |
|---|---|---|---|
| AEBS + stability | AIS-162 / IS 11852:2019 | **1 Jan 2027** | **1 Oct 2027** [S4] |
| LDWS | AIS-188 | 1 Oct 2027 | 1 Jan 2028 [S4] |
| DDAWS / BSIS / MOIS | AIS-184/186/187 (rule 125Q) | 1 Oct 2027 | 1 Jan 2028 [S4] |

Independently corroborated outside this repo: Fortune India reports AEBS and stability
control mandatory from **1 October 2027**, and BSIS/MOIS/DDAWS/LDWS from **1 January 2028**, [S4]
notified vide **GSR 834(E) dated 11 November 2025**. [S8] MoRTH's own AIS register still
lists AIS-162 with status **"Draft"**. [S10]

So there is no April 2026 mandate. There is no "regulatory floor" already live. The [S10]
document's opening argument, its "Why Act Now" close, and its ₹61,000 Cr demand framing all [S10]
rest on a superseded draft. [S1]

**This was already flagged.** Prior work in this repo lists as an explicit stop-claim:
"April 2026 mandate, G.S.R. 184(E) as final law." [S6] The June IM still makes both.

**Consequence beyond correctness:** the corrected timetable breaks the timing story. ASIC
tapeout is Q1–mid 2027 with volume qualified silicon in H1 2028 — *after* the 1 Oct 2027 [S6]
all-models deadline. [S7] The ASIC is a margin event, not a market-entry event, and 1 Jan
2027 line-fit is already unwinnable because design-in runs 18–30 months and it is now [S7]
August 2026. [S7]

### C2. The ₹1 Cr defence revenue is misdescribed four ways

The IM states "₹1Cr Defence Revenue" on the cover, "₹1 Cr institutional defence revenue
RECEIVED Q1 2026" on p.15, "₹1Cr Defence Revenue Received" as a completed milestone on [S7]
p.18, and "₹1 Cr defence revenue already received" in the exit vectors on p.26. [S1]

Prior verified work finds: **₹23.01L delivered** (GeM Robot Training); **₹78.39L is L1 — [S1]
lowest bidder, not an award**; it sits in **Deepgrid Datacentre Pvt Ltd, not Deepgrid Semi**;
and IP on the delivered contract is **MCEME-owned under Clause 27**. The company's own BP-1A [S1]
states "none of it is trucking/ADAS revenue." [S5]

Four separate problems in one number: 78% of it is not revenue, it is in a different legal [S5]
entity than the one raising, the IP does not belong to the company, and none of it is ADAS.
A single diligence question retires it.

### C3. The competitor set omits every threat that prior work ranked highest

The IM names Mobileye, Bosch, Steradian, Horizon Robotics, Nvidia, Netrasemi, NXP,
Mindgrove, Aurora, Kodiak, ABB/Kalmar, ideaForge, Apptronik and Agility, and asserts on p.19
that Indian competition is a "genuine open lane… the one unit where 'no Indian competitor' is
unambiguously true." [S1]

Not named, and ranked as the real threats: [S4][S6][S11]

- **ZF WABCO** — ADAS already shipping as OE fitment with a leading Indian CV OEM, with an
  in-house Indian test track and homologation capability DeepGrid concedes it lacks. Prior
  work calls this the actual threat, "not Nvidia/Mobileye." [S4][S7]
- **Starkenn** — company-reported 2,500+ vehicles and 30% of revenue from government [S4] [S7]
  tenders, plus BrakeSafe radar AEBS; highest threat in the government/mining segment the IM
  treats as open. [S6]
- **drivebuddyAI** — $2.5M contracted across 3,000 heavy trucks (May 2026), scope to 10,000. [S6]
  No longer demo-only. [S6][S11]
- **bitsensing** — India retrofit ADAS kit launched Feb 2026 with an IKIO channel MoU. [S4]
- **Gahan AI**, **Aptiv**, **STRADVISION**, **Sterling Tools × MINIEYE**. [S11]

**Netrasemi's ₹107 Cr Series A also demotes "India-native" as a differentiator** — domestic [S11]
origin alone no longer distinguishes DeepGrid. [S5][S7]

### C4. The import price column cannot be sourced, and quoting it was already banned

p.14 prices Mobileye at ₹5–8L, Continental ₹8–12L, Nvidia ₹12–18L, Qualcomm ₹10–15L, [S5] [S7]
footnoted only as "public product specifications" with no citation. [S1]

**No public per-unit India price exists** for Mobileye EyeQ6, Bosch/Continental, Qualcomm
SA8295 or Nvidia Orin truck ADAS. Six of 27 rows in a prior evidence ledger are UNVERIFIED [S1]
and all six are import prices; bitsensing's "pricing agreed case by case" corroborates that
this is market structure, not a search failure — **automotive ADAS unit pricing is quoted,
not listed.** [S4] A prior deck made quoting such a figure an explicit banned action. [S7]

Stage 1 already showed the "3 to 7×" claim contradicts its own table (the range is 2.0×–9.0×). [S4] [S7]
[S1] Loaded with NRE at plan volumes, prior work puts the real advantage at **~2.3×**, and
notes that the same category error — comparing a competitor ASP to DeepGrid's die cost — is
what forced an earlier "774×" claim to be retracted. [S5]

### C5. The 88% gross margin is arithmetically unreachable [S5]

The IM projects 88% blended GM at FY2032. [S1] Prior analysis: **88.4% blended requires
~120% margin on all non-kit revenue. At 72% kit margin the honest blend is 77.2%.** [S5]

An 11-point GM error at FY2032 revenue of ₹1,388 Cr is roughly ₹150 Cr of gross profit, which [S5]
flows straight to the ₹536.6 Cr EBITDA line the deck leads with. [S1]
